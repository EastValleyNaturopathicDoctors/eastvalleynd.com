<h2>What Is Lyme Disease?</h2>

<p>Lyme disease is a bacterial infection caused by <em>Borrelia burgdorferi</em> that is most commonly transmitted through infected ticks. While Lyme disease was once believed to be isolated to the northeastern United States, it has now spread throughout much of the country, including the Southwest, and is increasingly recognized worldwide. Because Lyme bacteria can evade the immune system and testing may not always detect the infection, diagnosis is often much more complicated than many people realize.</p>

<p>Many people never remember being bitten by a tick or developing the classic "bullseye" rash. Others may experience symptoms weeks, months, or even years after their initial exposure. Lyme disease has earned the nickname "the great imitator" because it can mimic many other chronic illnesses, leading to delayed diagnosis and treatment.</p>

<h2>Common Lyme Disease Symptoms</h2>

<p>Lyme disease can affect multiple body systems, causing symptoms that vary from person to person. Common symptoms include:</p>

<ul>

  <li>Brain fog, memory problems, and difficulty concentrating</li>
  <li>Joint and muscle pain</li>
  <li>Headaches and neck stiffness</li>
  <li>Sleep disturbances and insomnia</li>
  <li>Anxiety, depression, and mood changes</li>
  <li>Digestive concerns</li>
  <li>Numbness, tingling, or nerve pain</li>
  <li>Heart palpitations</li>
  <li>Hormonal imbalances and adrenal dysfunction</li>
</ul>

<p>Many patients also develop symptoms from co-infections such as Babesia, Bartonella, Ehrlichia, Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV), Candida overgrowth, parasites, or mold toxicity, which may significantly increase the severity and complexity of their condition.</p>

<p>Early symptoms often include fatigue, fever, headaches, muscle aches, joint pain, swollen lymph nodes, and a characteristic bullseye rash. However, many people never develop the rash or do not remember being bitten by a tick.</p>

<p>Some individuals continue to experience symptoms long after their initial infection. Chronic inflammation, co-infections, immune dysfunction, gut imbalances, mold exposure, and other underlying factors may contribute to ongoing health concerns.</p>

<h3>Why is Lyme disease difficult to diagnose?</h3>
