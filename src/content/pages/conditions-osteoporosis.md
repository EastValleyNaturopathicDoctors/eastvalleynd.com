<h2>Comprehensive Osteoporosis Care at East Valley Naturopathic Doctors</h2>
Osteoporosis is a condition characterized by a decrease in bone mass and density, causing bones to become fragile and more susceptible to fractures. This occurs when bone is no longer replaced as quickly as it is removed by the body. Because bone loss often develops gradually and without symptoms, many people are unaware they have osteoporosis until a fracture occurs.

<h2>Understanding Osteoporosis</h2>
Healthy bones are constantly remodeling. Throughout life, the body breaks down old bone while creating new bone. Osteoporosis develops when this balance shifts and bone is removed faster than it can be replaced. As bone density decreases, bones become weaker and more fragile, increasing the likelihood of fractures, particularly in the:
<ul>
 	<li>Hip</li>
 	<li>Spine</li>
 	<li>Wrist</li>
 	<li>Other weight-bearing bones</li>
</ul>
Good bone density plays an essential role in maintaining mobility, independence, and overall quality of life.
<h2>Who Is at Risk for Osteoporosis?</h2>
Several factors can increase your risk of developing osteoporosis or experiencing fractures. Patients at higher risk include those with:
<ul>
 	<li>A family history of osteoporosis</li>
 	<li>Previous fractures</li>
 	<li>Smoking history</li>
 	<li>Rheumatoid arthritis or other inflammatory conditions</li>
 	<li>Poor exercise habits</li>
 	<li>Calcium deficiency</li>
 	<li>Vitamin D deficiency</li>
 	<li>Menopause and age-related bone loss</li>
</ul>
Maintenance of optimal bone health requires being both active and proactive. At East Valley Naturopathic Doctors, our physicians evaluate your personal risk factors and recommend individualized protocols designed to optimize bone health and significantly reduce your risk of fractures.
<h2>Common Symptoms of Osteoporosis</h2>
Osteoporosis is often called a "silent disease" because many people experience no symptoms until significant bone loss has occurred. As the condition progresses, symptoms may include:
<ul>
 	<li>Loss of height</li>
 	<li>Stooped posture</li>
 	<li>Back pain</li>
 	<li>Fragility fractures</li>
 	<li>Bones that break more easily than expected</li>
 	<li>Reduced mobility after fractures</li>
</ul>
Early screening allows treatment to begin before serious complications develop.

<h2>DEXA Bone Density Testing</h2>
One of the most important tools for evaluating bone health is the DEXA (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry) scan.

DEXA scanning is a safe, reliable, and non-invasive method for measuring bone mineral density, particularly in the hip and spine.

Bone density testing is recommended for patients who are at increased risk for osteoporosis. In many cases, obtaining a baseline scan around menopause is recommended because bone loss often accelerates during this time unless a previous fracture has already prompted earlier evaluation.

Following your initial scan, DEXA testing is generally recommended every 1 to 2 years, depending on your individual risk factors and treatment plan.
<h2>Bone Resorption Testing</h2>
Because changes in bone density occur gradually, waiting one to two years between DEXA scans may delay adjustments to treatment.

For this reason, Bone Resorption Tests can provide valuable additional information.

These urine tests measure the rate at which bone is actively breaking down.

Bone resorption testing can often be performed during your initial visit and repeated approximately four months later to determine whether bone loss has slowed or stabilized following treatment recommendations.

If ongoing bone loss is detected, your physician at East Valley Naturopathic Doctors can modify your treatment plan much sooner, allowing for a more proactive approach to protecting bone health.
<h2>Vitamin D Testing</h2>
Vitamin D plays a critical role in maintaining healthy bones.

Research has demonstrated a direct relationship between low Vitamin D levels and an increased risk of fractures. Unfortunately, Vitamin D deficiency is extremely common and often goes unnoticed.

A simple blood test can determine your Vitamin D level, allowing your physician to recommend the appropriate amount of supplementation based on your individual needs rather than relying on generalized dosing recommendations.
<h2>Measuring Inflammation with CRP</h2>
Chronic inflammation may contribute to accelerated bone loss.

A high-sensitivity C-Reactive Protein (CRP) blood test helps measure the level of inflammation occurring within the body.

Although CRP does not identify the exact source of inflammation, it provides valuable information that allows your physician to develop personalized strategies aimed at reducing inflammation, lowering CRP levels, and supporting healthier bone metabolism.
