<h2>A Whole-Person Approach to Skin Health</h2>

<p>Skin conditions can be frustrating, uncomfortable, and sometimes difficult to treat. They can also affect confidence and quality of life, particularly when symptoms are chronic or repeatedly return after treatment.</p>

<p>Conventional treatments can be valuable for controlling symptoms, but persistent skin problems may warrant a closer look at factors occurring elsewhere in the body.</p>

<p>Depending on the condition and your individual health history, your physician at East Valley Naturopathic Doctors may evaluate factors such as:</p>

<ul>

  <li>Food sensitivities or intolerances</li>

  <li>Immune function</li>
  <li>Digestive health</li>
  <li>Nutritional deficiencies</li>
  <li>Environmental exposures</li>
  <li>Stress and lifestyle factors</li>
  <li>Current medications and skin care products</li>
</ul>

<p>Not every skin condition has the same cause, and not every patient requires the same testing or treatment. A comprehensive evaluation helps us determine which factors are most relevant to you.</p>

<h2>Skin Conditions We Treat</h2>

<h3>Acne</h3>

<p><a href="/conditions/acne/">Acne</a> can affect patients well beyond the teenage years. Breakouts may occur on the face, neck, chest, shoulders, or back and can range from occasional clogged pores to persistent inflammatory or cystic acne.</p>

<p>While excess oil and clogged pores contribute to acne, other factors can influence the frequency and severity of breakouts. These may include hormonal changes, inflammation, diet, stress, skin care products, and certain medications.</p>

<p>At East Valley Naturopathic Doctors in Mesa, acne treatment begins by considering the pattern of your breakouts as well as your overall health.</p>

<p>Depending on your needs, an individualized acne treatment plan may address:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Hormonal factors</li>
  <li>Nutrition and dietary patterns</li>
  <li>Inflammatory triggers</li>
  <li>Nutrient deficiencies</li>
  <li>Digestive health</li>
  <li>Appropriate topical skin care</li>
  <li>Lifestyle factors</li>
</ul>

<p>Our goal is not only to improve active breakouts but also to identify factors that may be contributing to recurring acne.</p>

<h3>Eczema</h3>

<p><a href="/conditions/eczema/">Eczema</a> is an inflammatory skin condition that commonly causes dry, itchy, irritated, or inflamed skin. Symptoms can range from small patches of irritation to more extensive flare-ups that interfere with sleep, comfort, and everyday activities.</p>

<p>For some patients, eczema appears periodically. For others, it becomes a chronic condition with repeated cycles of improvement and flare-ups.</p>

<p>Potential triggers vary significantly from person to person and may include environmental allergens, irritating skin products, stress, temperature changes, immune responses, and dietary factors.</p>

<p>Because eczema can have multiple contributing factors, East Valley Naturopathic Doctors takes an individualized approach to evaluation and treatment. Your physician may consider your diet, allergy history, digestive health, immune function, environmental exposures, and other possible contributors when developing your treatment plan.</p>

<p>Treatment may focus on reducing irritation and inflammation while identifying and minimizing personal triggers whenever possible.</p>

<h2>Identifying Potential Causes of Chronic Skin Problems</h2>

<h3>Food Intolerances and Diet</h3>

<p>For certain patients, dietary factors may contribute to inflammatory symptoms.</p>

<p>When clinically appropriate, your physician may discuss dietary changes or additional evaluation to determine whether particular foods could be contributing to your symptoms.</p>

<p>Rather than unnecessarily eliminating long lists of foods, the goal is to develop a nutritional strategy based on your health history and individual needs.</p>

<h3>Hormonal Health</h3>

<p>Hormonal fluctuations can have a significant effect on the skin, particularly with acne.</p>

<p>Breakouts that develop or worsen around puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, perimenopause, menopause, or other periods of hormonal change may warrant further evaluation.</p>

<p>At East Valley Naturopathic Doctors, we can consider hormonal health within the context of your overall symptoms rather than viewing your skin in isolation.</p>

<h3>Inflammation and Immune Function</h3>

<p>Inflammation is a normal part of the body's immune response, but ongoing or poorly regulated inflammation can contribute to a variety of health concerns.</p>

<p>Inflammatory skin conditions may prompt your physician to consider factors affecting immune balance and inflammation throughout the body.</p>

<h3>Nutrition and Micronutrients</h3>

<p>Healthy skin requires adequate vitamins, minerals, essential fatty acids, protein, and other nutrients.</p>

<p>Dietary restrictions, poor absorption, digestive concerns, and other factors may influence nutritional status. When indicated, laboratory testing can help identify deficiencies that may need to be addressed as part of a broader treatment program.</p>

<h3>Digestive Health</h3>

<p>The digestive and immune systems are closely connected, which is why digestive health may be considered when evaluating certain chronic skin complaints.</p>

<p>If you experience skin problems alongside digestive symptoms or suspected food reactions, your physician may recommend further evaluation based on your individual history.</p>

<h2>Testing for Persistent Skin Conditions</h2>

<p>Testing is not necessary for every patient with acne, eczema, or another skin complaint. However, when symptoms suggest that something beyond the skin may be contributing to the problem, laboratory evaluation can provide additional information.</p>

<p>Depending on your symptoms and medical history, your naturopathic physician may recommend appropriate laboratory testing to evaluate areas such as nutritional status, hormones, inflammation, or other suspected contributors.</p>

<p>At East Valley Naturopathic Doctors, testing is used to answer specific clinical questions and help create a more individualized treatment plan.</p>

<h2>When Should You See a Doctor About a Skin Problem?</h2>

<p>Consider scheduling an evaluation if you have a skin condition that:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Continues to return</li>
  <li>Has not responded adequately to previous treatment</li>
  <li>Is becoming increasingly uncomfortable</li>
  <li>Causes significant itching, irritation, or inflammation</li>
  <li>Appears to be associated with food, hormonal, or environmental triggers</li>
  <li>Is affecting your confidence or quality of life</li>
  <li>Involves a new or changing skin lesion</li>
  <li>May require an in-office procedure</li>
</ul>

<p>Sudden or severe skin reactions, rapidly spreading rashes, signs of serious infection, or skin symptoms accompanied by difficulty breathing or swelling require prompt medical attention.</p>
