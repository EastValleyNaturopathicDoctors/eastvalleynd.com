<h2>Understanding Arthritis</h2>

<p>Arthritis is not a single condition but a broad term used to describe inflammation and degeneration within the joints. While there are many different types of arthritis, including osteoarthritis and inflammatory forms of arthritis, chronic inflammation plays a significant role in many cases.</p>

<p>Over time, inflammation can damage cartilage, ligaments, tendons, and other supporting structures within the joints. As this process continues, patients often experience increasing pain, stiffness, swelling, and reduced mobility.</p>

<p>Because every patient experiences arthritis differently, successful treatment begins with understanding what is contributing to the inflammation and joint dysfunction.</p>

<h2>Common Symptoms of Arthritis</h2>

<p>Arthritis symptoms can develop gradually or appear suddenly depending on the underlying cause. Common symptoms include:</p>

<ul>
<li>Joint pain</li>
<li>Joint stiffness, especially after periods of rest</li>
<li>Swelling around affected joints</li>
<li>Reduced range of motion</li>
<li>Tenderness</li>
<li>Difficulty performing everyday activities</li>
<li>Weakness around affected joints</li>
<li>Chronic inflammation</li>
<li>Pain that worsens with activity</li>
</ul>

<p>Many patients also notice that symptoms fluctuate depending on stress levels, diet, activity, weather changes, or previous injuries.</p>
