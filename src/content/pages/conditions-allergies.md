<p>Allergic Rhinitis or runny nose is perhaps one of the most irritating medical complaints. Constant rubbing, blowing, and chaffing of the nose, due to chronic rhinitis, can also be treated without the use of medications. Patients often report red and irritated eyes and frequent sinus infections due to chronic nasal inflammation which increases the risk of infections.</p>

<p>Rhinitis can result from exposure to common and well-known airborne triggers. These include dust mites, cockroaches, dog dander, cat dander, trees, weeds, grasses, and molds, and sometimes perfumes and fragrances. These are found plentiful in the outdoors and can concentrate indoors, particularly in the carpet and ductwork of a home. These are evaluated using skin and serum blood tests through common labs.</p>

<p>The second cause that must be evaluated and treated includes <a href="/conditions/igg-food-intolerance/">IgG reactions to commonly consumed foods</a>. Individuals can become allergic and have delayed responses which also cause chronic rhinitis and increase the risk of chronic sinus infections. These reactions often occur in the gastrointestinal tract but produce rhinitis symptoms distally in the nose and sinuses causing inflammation and congestion. Elimination of this allergy symptom requires avoidance of the IgG-offending food(s). The most common foods that cause histamine and mucous production throughout the whole respiratory tract include dairy, bread (particularly gluten), and yeast. Often a candida cleanse with the concomitant elimination of the dairy and bread can do immediate wonders for chronic sinus and nasal congestion and infections. Testing is also available for this type of allergy response.</p>

<p>Combining IgE and IgG allergy testing provides the most accurate prediction of sources to cause rhinitis. Treatment is nearly 100% successful with combined testing and specific food elimination, and desensitization with the sublingual immunotherapy.</p>

They produce all the same symptoms except fail to produce a specific antibody response. The good news is, that one simple blood test can differentiate between them all.
