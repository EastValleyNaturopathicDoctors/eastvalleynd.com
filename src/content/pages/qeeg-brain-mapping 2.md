<p>Quantitative EEG (QEEG), often called a brain map, is a noninvasive tool that measures the electrical activity your brain produces and compares it against a normative database to identify patterns that differ from a healthy baseline. At <a href="/">East Valley Naturopathic Doctors</a> in Mesa, Arizona, QEEG brain mapping serves as the diagnostic foundation for creating individualized Neurofeedback treatment plans that are tailored to each patient's unique brain function.</p>

<p>Electroencephalography itself isn't new. Richard Caton first identified electrical activity in the brain in 1875, and Hans Berger recorded it in humans in 1929, showing that brain rhythms originate from neurons and change with age, stimulation, and brain chemistry. Despite the later arrival of MRI, CT, PET, and SPECT scans, EEG has remained a trusted clinical tool because it provides valuable information without exposing patients to radiation.</p>

<h2>How Brain Mapping Works</h2>

<p>QEEG measures electrical patterns at the scalp, generated by roughly 20 billion neurons, using 19 channels of data collected through small sensors or a comfortable cap fitted with conductive gel. Data is recorded for approximately 3 to 5 minutes with your eyes closed and then repeated with your eyes open. The entire appointment typically lasts 30 to 45 minutes.</p>

<p>A computer analyzes the data with far greater precision than visual interpretation alone, comparing your results to a large normative database to identify meaningful deviations. This provides an objective, scientific foundation that allows the providers at East Valley Naturopathic Doctors to develop a personalized Neurofeedback treatment plan instead of relying on guesswork.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Is a QEEG the same as an EEG at a hospital?</h3>

<p>They use the same underlying technology, but a QEEG adds sophisticated computer analysis that compares your brainwave patterns to a normative database, something a standard clinical EEG typically doesn't provide.</p>

<h3>Does QEEG hurt?</h3>

<p>No. The procedure is completely painless. Sensors are placed against the scalp, sometimes using a cap and conductive gel, and there is no radiation involved.</p>

<h3>Can QEEG diagnose ADHD on its own?</h3>

<p>No. QEEG provides valuable objective data that contributes to an ADHD evaluation and is often paired with QbCheck for a more complete assessment. A diagnosis is made by your physician after considering your full medical history, symptoms, and test results.</p>
