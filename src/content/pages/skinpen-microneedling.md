<h3><strong>SkinPen Procedure</strong></h3>
Our licensed aesthetician will cleanse the face, and apply numbing cream before the procedure. The cream is allowed to rest on the face for 10-15 minutes. <h2>Facts About Our Aging Skin</h2>
Aging is not something we can avoid 100% even if we choose to do everything just right. But our choices in regards to how we eat, manage our stress, and take care of ourselves can slow the aging process internally and even show up externally in the quality of our skin. Research has laid out in a very clear and precise manner the leading causes of general aging as well as the causes of aging skin.  These singular and accumulative effects of aging include free radical damage, chronic inflammation, collagen degradation, nutrient deficiencies, infrequent autophagy, and of course just aging itself.Free radicals are highly reactive molecules with unpaired electrons that can cause significant damage to skin cells, contributing to the aging process. Free radicals seek stability by stealing electrons from other molecules, such as proteins, lipids, and DNA in skin cells. This process, known as oxidative stress, can damage the structural integrity of cells, leading to premature aging. Oxidative stress from free radicals can trigger an inflammatory response in the skin. Chronic low-level inflammation accelerates the aging process by further degrading collagen and other essential proteins. The skin is made up of 70-80% collagen, which is a protein that gives skin its firmness and elasticity. Free radicals can break down collagen fibers, leading to wrinkles, fine lines, and sagging skin. And free radicals can damage the lipids in the skin's barrier, reducing its ability to retain moisture. This leads to dryness, rough texture, and a dull appearance, all of which are common signs of aging.Though free radicals can contribute to inflammation, chronic inflammation arises from many other sources as well. Inflammation damages aging skin by accelerating the breakdown of collagen and elastin, the proteins responsible for skin's firmness and elasticity. When the skin experiences chronic inflammation, whether from environmental factors like UV exposure and pollution, or internal factors such as stress and poor diet, it triggers the release of inflammatory molecules. These molecules can degrade collagen fibers and slow down the skin's natural repair processes, leading to wrinkles, sagging, and a loss of youthful resilience. Additionally, inflammation can increase the production of melanin, causing uneven skin tone and hyperpigmentation. Over time, this ongoing damage compromises the skin's structure and accelerates visible signs of aging.Collagen degradation in aging skin is primarily caused by a combination of intrinsic aging processes and external factors. As we age, the body’s natural production of collagen slows down, leading to a gradual decline in skin firmness and elasticity. This intrinsic aging is compounded by external factors such as UV radiation from sun exposure, which accelerates collagen breakdown through the production of free radicals and the activation of enzymes called matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) that degrade collagen fibers. Other factors like pollution, smoking, and chronic inflammation further contribute to collagen degradation by generating oxidative stress and damaging the skin's structural proteins. Together, these factors weaken the skin's support system, resulting in wrinkles, sagging, and a loss of youthful texture.Nutrient deficiencies can significantly accelerate skin aging by impairing the skin's ability to repair and protect itself and accelerate the visible signs of aging. Here are some key nutrients whose deficiencies can contribute to aging skin:<strong>Vitamin C</strong>: Essential for collagen synthesis, vitamin C deficiency leads to reduced collagen production, resulting in wrinkles, sagging, and loss of skin elasticity. It also acts as an antioxidant, protecting the skin from oxidative stress and free radical damage.<strong>Vitamin E</strong>: As a powerful antioxidant, vitamin E protects the skin from oxidative damage caused by UV radiation and environmental pollutants. A deficiency can lead to increased skin damage, dryness, and premature aging.<strong>Vitamin A (and Beta-Carotene)</strong>: Vitamin A, particularly in the form of retinoids, is crucial for skin cell turnover and repair. A deficiency can cause rough, dry skin, and contribute to the formation of wrinkles and fine lines.<strong>Zinc</strong>: Zinc plays a role in skin healing and inflammation control. A deficiency can impair wound healing, increase susceptibility to skin infections, and exacerbate conditions like acne and eczema, contributing to aging skin.<strong>Omega-3 Fatty Acids</strong>: These essential fatty acids help maintain the skin's lipid barrier, keeping it hydrated and plump. A deficiency can lead to dry, inflamed skin and contribute to the appearance of fine lines and wrinkles.<strong>Protein</strong>: Adequate protein intake is necessary for collagen production and skin repair. A protein deficiency can lead to a loss of skin elasticity, increased wrinkling, and a dull complexion.<strong>B Vitamins</strong>: Deficiencies in B vitamins, particularly B3 (niacin), B5 (pantothenic acid), and B7 (biotin), can lead to dry, flaky skin, and increase the likelihood of inflammation, which accelerates skin aging.Autophagy is the natural cleaning-out process that starts when our cells are under stress when our cells are under stress or deprived of nutrients. Autophagy is a cellular process that plays a critical role in supporting aging skin by removing damaged cellular components and promoting the regeneration of healthy cells. It is a natural cleaning out process that starts when our cells are under stress or deprived of nutrients. Autophagy is activated when we reduce total daily calories(caloric deficit) or eat in a smaller window (intermittent fasting) avoiding food for a minimum of 12-16 hours. As we age, the efficiency of autophagy declines, leading to the accumulation of damaged proteins and organelles within skin cells, which can result in wrinkles, loss of elasticity, and a dull complexion. Thus as we age we need to eat less generally and eat less often to help our bodies naturally rejuvenate. By clearing out these cellular "waste" products, autophagy helps maintain skin integrity, boosts collagen production, and reduces inflammation, which are essential for youthful, resilient skin and aging.
