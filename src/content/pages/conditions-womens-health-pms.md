<h2>Understanding PMS</h2>

<p>Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) typically begins one to two weeks before your period and improves within the first few days of menstruation. While symptoms vary from person to person, they often become more severe when hormone imbalances are left untreated.</p>

<p>Common PMS symptoms include:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Breast tenderness</li>
  <li>Bloating</li>
  <li>Irritability and mood changes</li>
  <li>Food cravings</li>
  <li>Insomnia</li>
  <li>Headaches</li>
  <li>Anxiety or depression</li>
  <li>Abdominal discomfort</li>
  <li>Swelling in the legs</li>
</ul>

<p>Many women with PMS experience estrogen dominance, meaning estrogen remains higher than progesterone during the second half of the menstrual cycle. This hormonal imbalance can contribute to both physical and emotional symptoms.</p>

<h2>Natural Treatment for Painful Periods</h2>

<p>Painful periods, also known as dysmenorrhea, affect millions of women each month. For some, menstrual cramps become severe enough to interfere with work, school, or other everyday activities. The good news is that effective treatment often starts with identifying the underlying cause.</p>

<p>One common reason for menstrual pain is excess production of prostaglandins. These naturally occurring chemicals help the uterus contract during menstruation, but when produced in excess, they can lead to intense cramping and inflammation.</p>

<h3>What causes PMS?</h3>

<p>PMS is commonly caused by hormonal imbalances during the second half of the menstrual cycle, particularly when estrogen levels remain higher than progesterone. Other contributing factors may include nutrient deficiencies, poor detoxification, gut health issues, and chronic inflammation.</p>

<h3>Can painful periods be treated naturally?</h3>

<p>Yes. Many women experience significant improvement with natural treatments such as nutritional therapy, hormone-balancing herbs, omega-3 fish oil, acupuncture, and lifestyle changes. Treatment is most effective when the underlying cause is properly identified.</p>

<h3>When should I see a doctor for PMS or painful periods?</h3>

<p>If your symptoms interfere with work, school, relationships, or daily activities, or if over-the-counter medications are no longer providing relief, it's time to seek an evaluation. Identifying and treating the root cause early can help prevent symptoms from becoming more severe over time.</p>
