<h2>Understanding Menopause</h2>

<p>Menopause is officially defined as going 12 consecutive months without a menstrual period. However, symptoms often begin years before menopause during perimenopause and may continue for five to ten years afterward.</p>

<p>As women age, the ovaries gradually produce less estrogen and progesterone. These hormonal changes can affect nearly every system in the body, leading to symptoms that vary in severity from person to person.</p>

<p>Common menopause symptoms include:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Hot flashes and night sweats</li>
  <li>Mood swings and anxiety</li>
  <li>Insomnia and fatigue</li>
  <li>Weight gain</li>
  <li>Low libido</li>
  <li>Vaginal dryness</li>
  <li>Frequent urinary tract infections (UTIs)</li>
  <li>Memory or concentration changes</li>
  <li>Joint aches</li>
  <li>Skin and hair changes</li>
  <li>Headaches</li>
</ul>

<p>Hormonal changes are only part of the picture. Thyroid function, adrenal health, stress levels, digestion, and nutrition all play important roles in how women experience menopause.</p>

<h2>Bioidentical Hormone Therapy</h2>

<p>Women with moderate to severe symptoms who do not find adequate relief through natural therapies may benefit from bioidentical hormone replacement therapy (BHRT).</p>

<p>Unlike standardized hormone medications, compounded bioidentical hormones are customized to match each woman's specific needs. Topical hormone creams are often preferred because they may offer a lower risk of blood clotting compared to some oral estrogen therapies.</p>

<h3>Estrogen Therapy</h3>

<p>Bioidentical estrogen therapy can help reduce:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Hot flashes</li>
  <li>Night sweats</li>
  <li>Mood changes</li>
  <li>Bone loss associated with osteoporosis</li>
</ul>

<p>Many customized formulas combine estradiol and estriol, two forms of bioidentical estrogen that closely resemble the hormones naturally produced by the body.</p>

<h3>Progesterone Therapy</h3>

<p>Progesterone is commonly prescribed alongside estrogen to help protect the uterus. It also offers additional benefits, including:</p>

<ul>
  <li>Improved sleep</li>
  <li>Reduced anxiety</li>
  <li>Relief from mild hot flashes</li>
  <li>Calming support through GABA receptor activity</li>
</ul>

<p>Depending on individual needs, progesterone may be prescribed as an oral capsule or topical cream.</p>

<h3>Testosterone Therapy</h3>

<p>Some women develop low testosterone levels during menopause, contributing to decreased libido and low energy. Carefully monitored testosterone therapy may improve sexual health and overall well-being.</p>

<p>Because excessive testosterone can cause unwanted side effects such as acne, facial hair growth, irritability, or hair loss, dosing should always be individualized and closely monitored.</p>

<h3>Vaginal Estrogen Therapy</h3>

<p>Even women receiving systemic hormone therapy may experience vaginal dryness, irritation, painful intercourse, or recurring urinary tract infections after menopause.</p>

<p>Localized vaginal estriol therapy helps maintain healthy vaginal tissue, improve comfort, and reduce the risk of recurrent infections while supporting overall vaginal health.</p>
